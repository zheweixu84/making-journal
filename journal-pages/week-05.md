## Reflective Proposal

----

In the past few weeks, I experienced multiple different data experiments. And looking back at the making journal, what I’ve been most enjoying is the experiment in data visualisation and using the data as a creative tool. When the data gets through the screens and becomes a physical object that can be felt, it’s so meaningful. The experiment showed that, instead of scrolling through a data chart, showing the data as a flow slowly and visibly, will be more attractive and meaningful. What I have delved into is how the distortion and layering, reveal uncertainty and subjectivity. Thai is compatible with the data humanism and data physicalisation. My practical work aligns with Giorgia Lupi’s documentary, I use her ideas to record visualise the data. I want to carry forward the human-centred idea into my project and keep it in a more resolved physical form. Focusing on the material, interaction with people and how these will change the aspect of data or the relationship between people and the others.

Based on the previous, my project will explore how social interaction will be recorded and reconstructed by a speculative data system. What I find interesting is that a relationship is not easy to visualise or record. But if this is possible, how will the relationship be recorded and what information can be shown through the data. In this imagined scenario, every interaction will be automatically transformed into a physical receipt, turning the human contact into a transaction system of documentation. To validate this idea, I will collect a small dataset over 4 days. Records every social interaction that is meaningful or useful. Every record will include who, type and time. All this information will be represented by a symbol or  shown on the receipts. The data will be intentionally small and real to make a closer reflection rather than a larger-scale analysis. These receipts will be arranged as a hanging scroll or wall installation. I gained this idea from the discussion about live data, the way of creating an installation to represent a dataset is inspiring.

The audience can experience the work by moving around the installation and directly engaging with the receipts, reordering them or feeling them, and comparing information. This will not only be an instantiation that can be viewed only, but also through the interactions to transform from a solid dataset to a reconfigurable system. The meaning will not be limited to itself, it will be fluid and unstable depending on the physical manipulation. This process is revealing how easily people make assumptions from limited or fragmented data. Even though the data is incomplete or reductive, the audience can still imagine the related people’s personalities. This conflict encourages people to think about and understand information beyond what is explicitly presented.

The starting point of this project is to question what is lost when human relationships transition into a system and measurements, everything is marked with a ‘price’. Aim to create a shift from familiar daily objects to strangeness in the social connection. This project reflects on the data system not only changing how we record our lives but also how we understand and value social connections and interactions.
