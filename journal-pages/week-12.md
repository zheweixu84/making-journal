## Project Statement

In my installation, "Receipts of Relationships," I explore how personal social interaction can be reconstructed through a data system that transforms human contact into physical, transactional evidence. The work is presented as a timeline of hanging thermal receipts, where the material's ephemeral, fragile nature reflects the social connection. The data for this project is drawn from my own life over a four-day period, during which I recorded 40 meaningful conversations. Each receipt documents the specific time, a coded identity to preserve the participant's privacy, the emotional tone represented by icons, and a quantified value ranging from -10 to 10 based on the interaction’s impact on my mood.

This work addresses a speculative future scenario where technology, such as biological chips or smart systems, automatically documents every human contact as a transaction, marking our most personal moments with a systemic "price". By using the familiar form of a commercial receipt, I critically engage with how complex human bonds are reduced to simplified, measurable data points. Through physical layering and overlapping, the installation reveals the inherent uncertainty and subjectivity of data—reminding us that no single record can capture the whole story.

I invite you to move through this installation not just as a viewer, but as an active participant, reading, touching, and reordering these fragments to reconstruct your own narratives. You are also encouraged to use the provided software to generate your own "receipt of connection," adding your record to this fluid, reconfigurable system. Ultimately, I want this work to prompt reflection on how we record and value our social bonds, challenging you to find what is "real" in a world where relationships have become a system of measurement.
